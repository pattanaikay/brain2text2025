Active Experiments & Research Plan

Two concurrent lines of work — Brain2Text multi-architecture ablations (WER reduction toward <10%) and a memory-augmented on-device BCI for ALS / brainstem-stroke patients (DietCorp-ZenBrain on Coral).

Project I · Brain2Text 2025 · Multi-Architecture Ablations Project II · DietCorp-ZenBrain · Edge Deployment Study
Research Statement  ·   · 

Neural speech decoding for patients with ALS or brainstem stroke — conditions that sever motor pathways while leaving cognition intact — is the central problem motivating both projects documented here. The clinical goal is a speech brain–computer interface that restores real-time communication from intracortical recordings, without cloud dependency, on hardware the patient already owns.

Two parallel research threads pursue this goal. The first — Brain2Text 2025 — systematically ablates the architectural design space of neural decoders: from classical sequence models (RNNs, LSTMs, GRUs) through modern attention-based architectures (Transformers, Mamba) and state-space models, to audio-pretrained, vision-pretrained, and multimodal LLMs. The primary aims are (a) to replicate and surpass current state-of-the-art decoding performance on the Brain-to-Text Benchmark '24, and (b) to identify which architectural priors drive the performance gap — enabling principled design decisions rather than blind architectural search.

The second — DietCorp-ZenBrain — investigates whether a compact, well-designed decoder can be deployed on consumer hardware (phones, laptops) using open-source quantised inference, and whether a neuroscience-inspired multi-tier memory hierarchy can sustain accurate decoding across days without recalibration sessions.

The deeper intellectual question threading both projects: when a neural network learns to decode motor-cortex signals, what has it actually learned about how the motor system encodes speech intention — and which computational structures best reflect that biology?

Track A · Research Deep-Dive
Investigating Multimodal Alignment in Neural Decoding: Audio vs. Visual Pretraining

Initial observations indicate that audio-pretrained LLMs outperform text-only baselines when decoding neural signals via an MLP projector. The experiments in Track A are designed to isolate the root cause through three controlled ablations, then characterise the modality gap more deeply through representational similarity analysis and temporal boundary synchronisation.

Phase 1 — Isolating the Audio Pretraining Advantage
Hypothesis Premise Ablation Strategy Success Metric
H1 · Phoneme Prior Audio models are robust to sub-word noise, allowing phonetic reconstruction from degraded signals. Inject embedding-level Gaussian noise/masking into both audio and text LLMs. WER/CER degradation curves under increasing noise
H2 · Spoken Language Prior Neural data reflects the statistical distribution of spoken language (shorter syntax, repetitions). Train a text-only LLM purely on spoken transcripts and swap into the decoding pipeline. BLEU/METEOR score parity with the audio baseline
H3 · Embedding Alignment The geometric topology of an audio latent space naturally resembles cortical semantic representations. Extract raw embeddings and compute representational similarity before MLP projection. Higher RSA / SVCCA scores for the audio model
Phase 2 — The Modality Gap (Heard vs. Seen)

To determine whether the brain's internal semantic convergence zones map more naturally to continuous auditory manifolds or spatial visual spaces, the pipeline leverages both JEPA and non-JEPA architectures.

Part A — JEPA-Based Latent Alignment

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) are well-suited for modelling high-dimensional brain data because they prioritise latent predictability over pixel/waveform reconstruction.

  • Modality Latent Alignment: Project identical fMRI/EEG data (captured during combined audio-visual stimuli) into frozen Audio-JEPA and Vision-JEPA spaces. Lower L2/cosine distance from the neural embeddings to the target space indicates the dominant modality.
  • Distributional Regularisation: Apply LeJEPA-style variance-covariance regularisation to the neural encoder's output. Forcing the neural data into an isotropic Gaussian shape prior to projection tests whether structural misalignment — rather than missing data — is the primary bottleneck.
Part B — Non-JEPA Manifold Topology

Contrastive models (CLAP, CLIP) prioritise global semantics; generative/autoregressive models (Whisper, ViT, Audio-LLMs) prioritise local sequential dependencies.

  • Intrinsic Dimensionality & Procrustes Analysis: Use Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) or Procrustes analysis to compare the raw neural manifold directly against CLAP/CLIP and Audio-LLM/Vision-LLM spaces without training a projector.
  • Representational Granularity: Measure alignment at varying levels of abstraction (single word vs. full narrative) to determine whether audio models win because they natively preserve temporal narrative context better than visual spaces.
Phase 3 — Temporal Dynamics via Selective Decoding (Neural Drift)

To validate that the targeted latent space captures genuine semantic meaning rather than temporal noise, the pipeline tracks Neural Drift — continuous shifts in the brain's internal representational state — through the latent space.

  1. Tracking State Shifts: Measure the continuous cosine distance between sequential neural vectors. A spike above a strict statistical threshold indicates a fundamental shift in the brain's internal representational state.
  2. Event Boundary Synchronisation: Correlate the timestamps of neural drift spikes with independent stimulus boundaries:
    • Visual boundaries: camera cuts, distinct optical flow changes, scene transitions.
    • Auditory boundaries: phoneme onsets, speaker changes, musical cues.
  3. Determining the Anchor Modality: If neural drift correlates primarily with visual boundaries, cortical representation is visually anchored — favouring Vision-Language target spaces. If it correlates with acoustic onsets (even during static visual frames), it confirms that higher-level semantics are structured sequentially like audio, cementing the architectural advantage of audio pretraining.

A memory-augmented intracortical speech BCI targeting ALS and brainstem-stroke patients. The base model is DietCorp-Compact; the extension adds a ZenBrain-inspired multi-tier memory hierarchy to improve test-time adaptation under day-to-day electrode distribution shift. The study characterises the composite system's behaviour under realistic edge-deployment constraints using Google's Coral platform as a simulation instrument.

Coral is an instrument, not a deployment target. Coral is used as a simulation microscope — a representative, instrumentable edge-NPU substrate that characterises how DietCorp (and memory-augmented versions) behave under realistic edge-deployment constraints: int8 quantisation, static shapes, limited control flow, bounded on-chip memory, and no on-device backprop — without committing to any specific production chip. The eventual deployment class is phones and laptops (Apple Neural Engine, Qualcomm Hexagon, Google Tensor TPU).

Analogy: Coral is to this project what a wind tunnel is to an airframe designer — you don't fly a plane in a wind tunnel, but the data you collect tells you what will fly. The primary deliverable is a Hardware Requirements Spec (a falsifiable spec sheet any candidate deployment substrate can be matched against), not a shipping decision.

1. The Ask

Clinical motivation

ALS and brainstem stroke destroy motor pathways while leaving cognition intact. A clinically useful BCI needs all four simultaneously:

  1. Low WER — text the patient and their family can actually read.
  2. Streaming, real-time decoding — output appears as the patient attempts to speak.
  3. On-device feasibility — runs on hardware the patient already owns, no cloud dependency.
  4. Robustness to distribution shift — neural recordings drift day-to-day; the model adapts without re-calibration sessions.

Most published BCI work optimises (1) at the expense of (2)–(4). This thesis is built around the four-objective version of the problem.

Why this architecture: the clinical constraints

High-performance speech neuroprostheses have historically relied on deep GRUs paired with large language models. While accurate on benchmarks, they carry two structural flaws that limit real-world clinical deployment:

  • Processing redundancy. Because GRUs have a lossy memory architecture, they require heavily overlapping temporal inputs — 87.5% overlap in standard configurations — creating immense computational waste and early overfitting on sparse clinical datasets.
  • Susceptibility to neural drift. Physical shifts at the electrode interface (micro-movements, impedance changes over days) progressively alter the recorded signal statistics. Uncalibrated decoders break down completely within a week: in controlled studies, WER climbs from 22.7% on day one to 66.5% WER over eight held-out days without adaptation.

DietCorp-Compact is designed around both constraints:

  • Causal Transformers with non-overlapping patches replace the GRU. The architecture processes entirely non-overlapping 100 ms patches of neural data, eliminating redundant computation and reducing parameter count by 83% and peak memory by 52% relative to the GRU baseline.
  • High-volume time-masking (~53% of temporal patches masked per training trial) prevents overfitting to noisy biological datasets by forcing the network to discover structural features that generalise across electrode configurations — without changing the inference path.
  • DietCORP TTA combats neural drift continuously and without storing historical data. After each decoded sentence, it generates 64 time-masked augmentations of the trial, refines the raw phonetic output into a pseudo-label via an n-gram LM, and takes exactly one AdamW gradient step — updating only the patch-embedding module at the network's input. This realigns shifted electrode signals while keeping the core language logic frozen. Cost: 18 ms per trial, 1.33 GiB peak GPU memory.

Proposed extension — ZenBrain-inspired memory layer

Source Bering, 2026  ·  arXiv:2604.23878. A neuroscience-grounded memory architecture for autonomous AI systems. ZenBrain structure 7 memory layers (working, short-term, episodic, semantic, procedural, core, cross-context) + 15 algorithms (Two-Factor synaptic edges, vmPFC-coupled FSRS, Simulation-Selection sleep, neuromodulator dynamics, TripleCopy stores, etc.). Hypothesis Giving DietCorp a layered memory hierarchy — with differentiated time constants, prioritisation, and consolidation rules — should improve TTA under distribution shift beyond single-shot per-trial adaptation. Important note ZenBrain is an LLM-agent memory orchestrator (knowledge-graph storage, BM25 + embedding retrieval, FSRS scheduling). ~14 of 15 components belong on the host CPU. The one on-NPU primitive earning its place is a fixed-shape working-memory KV cache co-located with DietCorp's patch-embedding module.

  2. (b) Can the augmented architecture (DietCorp + memory layer + TTA) be simulated on Coral given Edge TPU / Coral NPU constraints?
  3. (c) What metrics can be captured to make the comparison meaningful — and which require real silicon?

2. Feasibility Audit Findings Complete · 28 May 2026

Three landing points carry the rest of the work:

Finding 1 Both Coral substrates are useful, for complementary reasons. First-gen Edge TPU (USB Accelerator, Dev Board) is a closed Google ASIC with real silicon — gives wall-clock int8 latency numbers. The new Coral NPU is an open RISC-V reference IP with two public simulators (functional MPACT-CoralNPU + cycle-accurate Verilator) — gives cycle-level architectural visibility on an open toolchain. Both substrates feed the projection workstream as complementary data sources rather than competing options.
Finding 2 DietCorp's inference path is a clean fit on either substrate; the no-backprop-on-NPU limit is a non-issue. The model is small (~10 MB int8, ~36 MFLOPs per 100 ms patch), causal, streaming, dominated by matmul / softmax / LayerNorm. DietCORP's per-trial backward pass and Adam step run in host PyTorch driving the simulator — faithful to how production deployment would work on phone NPUs too.
Finding 3 ZenBrain stays largely host-side; the simulation work can be more exploratory than a shipping-product audit. ~14 of 15 ZenBrain components belong on host CPU. The one on-NPU primitive is the fixed-shape working-memory KV cache. Under simulation-as-instrument framing, more aggressive on-NPU placements can be tried — and when the simulator pushes back, those negative results are kept and reported as constraint findings for the spec sheet.

3. Seven Investigation Modes

All seven modes are active throughout the four stages — not sequential phases. Mode 7 (Hardware Requirements Projection) is the primary deliverable workstream.

Mode 1  ·  Architecture & Paper Reading

Close reading of DietCorp and ZenBrain to extract exact op graphs, parameter counts, training hyperparameters, and behavioural mechanisms. ZenBrain's appendix (B.1–B.11) is where the actual algorithms live — skipping it leads to treating ZenBrain as a neural network when it is an LLM-agent memory orchestrator.

Mode 2  ·  Toolchain & Platform Research

Coral NPU simulator stack (MPACT + Verilator), Edge TPU compiler, LiteRT / MLIR / IREE, int8 PTQ/QAT, op-compatibility lists. Track simulator maturity — the Coral NPU matrix execution unit is currently "under development and evaluation"; cycle counts from today's Verilator understate throughput production silicon will deliver. Document as explicit uncertainty bands.

Mode 3  ·  Op-Level Compatibility Analysis

For every op in DietCorp's forward pass — patch-embed (LayerNorm/Linear/LN), 5× Transformer blocks (causal MHA with T5-style relative-position bias, residual, FFN, GeLU), output head — does it map natively, fuse, fall back to vector execution, or force CPU offload? Determines the realistic latency floor. Repeated after any memory-tier addition.

Mode 4  ·  Placement Reasoning

For each ZenBrain layer and algorithm: NPU vs host CPU vs off-device. Structured argument from access patterns (sparse vs dense, static-shape vs dynamic), latency budget, memory size, and whether the subsystem is inside the streaming inference loop or runs at session boundaries. Iterative — try more aggressive on-NPU placements and let the simulator push back.

Mode 5  ·  Alternative Platform Scan

Controlled reference points so projected requirements aren't anchored to one substrate. Shortlist: first-gen Coral USB Accelerator (real int8 silicon), Coral NPU Verilator sim (cycle-level, open RISC-V), Apple Neural Engine via Core ML (phone-class sanity check). Phone-NPU run is not a deployment commitment — it validates that the eventual deployment-class device can plausibly hit the same envelope.

Mode 6  ·  Simulator vs Real-Silicon Separation

Strict classification of every metric: (i) simulator-free — total cycles, per-instruction traces, AXI traffic, on-chip memory residency; (ii) requires custom instrumentation — per-op timing, streaming throughput, full TTA loop (needs CSR cycle reads + host harness scripts); (iii) requires real hardware — wall-clock latency on production silicon, real power, thermal effects, and end-to-end TTA latency with weight reload. Prevents thesis over-claiming.

Mode 7  ·  Hardware Requirements Projection  (primary deliverable)

Derive a concrete spec sheet from simulation runs. Targets: sustained compute (MACs/sec at 10 Hz patch rate), peak compute (instantaneous MACs/cycle for heaviest op), on-chip memory (activation working set + quantized-weight residency), off-chip bandwidth (AXI/DRAM traffic per inference), op set (TFLite/MLIR ops + quantisation variants), latency budget breakdown (per-Transformer-block, per-attention-head, per-LayerNorm), TTA cost envelope (host-side gradient step + weight-reload latency), energy envelope (estimated from cycle counts, explicitly low-confidence until hardware-validated), memory-tier delta (per-variant delta on all above metrics vs vanilla DietCorp).

6. Risks & Open Decisions

Four decisions resolvable in a single advisor 1:1. None are blockers for Stage 1 or Stage 3 parallel toolchain setup.
Decision 1 Which Coral substrate gets primary simulator attention? Both are needed, but compute budget may force prioritisation. First-gen USB Accelerator turns around faster and gives wall-clock numbers directly. Coral NPU Verilator gives cycle-level architectural detail for a rigorous spec sheet. If forced to pick: Verilator first, USB second — the spec sheet is the primary deliverable.
Decision 2 How aggressively to escalate memory-tier variants in Stage 4? Variant A is safe and likely fits the streaming budget. Initial plan: A first, B if A leaves >20% of streaming budget unused, C as stress-test ablation regardless.
Decision 3 Toy dataset fidelity. Safe path: generate by perturbing recorded calibration-day trials from the public benchmark (preserves real noise statistics); use fully synthetic generative model only for early simulator-loop debugging.
Decision 4 Depth of the phone-NPU bonus run. Light-touch (one model, one device, single inference timing) is enough to support the deployment-class claim. Initial plan: light-touch in the thesis chapter; deep characterisation reserved for follow-on work.
Risk Coral NPU simulator maturity. MPACT + Verilator matrix execution unit is "under development and evaluation" per official docs. Mitigation: Stage 3b USB Accelerator provides real-silicon wall-clock validation; all simulator estimates carry an explicit uncertainty band in the spec sheet.

7. Questions for Advisor Review

Q1 QAT up-front vs wait for PTQ results? QAT roughly doubles training cost but is the standard way to recover 1–2% WER on Transformer quantisation. Starting QAT in parallel with Stage 1 would remove the 2-week contingency window — but only matters if PTQ degrades beyond 1% absolute.
Q2 Hardware Requirements Spec format: single combined document or per-variant spec sheets? Single document (vanilla DietCorp + Variant A + Variant B in one table) is tidier. Per-variant sheets are easier for a deployment engineer to act on independently.
Q3 Negative-result framing in Stage 4. The plan explicitly treats "memory variant X breaks the streaming budget" as a publishable input to the spec sheet. Does the simulation-as-instrument framing make negative results acceptable as primary contributions here?
